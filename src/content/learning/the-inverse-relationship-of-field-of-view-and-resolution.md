### What do you hope to accomplish with your images?

I remember the first day I got a "high-definition" television. I didn't really know what "1080p" HD meant, but I knew that it was so cool watching Michael Phelps win all those gold medals in his first Olympics, all the while feeling like the water from the swimming pool was about to splash me in the face. It was the first time I ever thought that THIS is the way TV should be... all this detail, with the additional byproduct of image sharpness (those text graphics are insanely easy to read) and color saturation (my old regular TV didn't look this vivid).

And now, almost 15 years later, I have a huge 4k television hanging on my wall. With a resolution of 2160p — my TV delivers 2160 pixels worth of information in the vertical dimension of the screen — I find myself watching 4k videos from YouTube of the most random of things just to enjoy the view.

Interestingly, if I watch a movie in "standard" definition and then again in high-definition, the detail amount increases, but it's not like I am seeing additional information outside of the camera's angle of view. And if I upgrade to a 4k view of the same movie, once again, the reality of the picture in front of me changes only by the increase in microscopic details I am able to see.

*This is akin to the astrophotographer feeling like he or she can get more detail in an image by swapping the camera to one with smaller pixels (assume the chip size is the same). The amount of information is multiplied substantially, but no new information is gained or lost in the amount of space the camera had captured.*

On the other hand, due to the better televisions we have at home, content providers have changed the way they shoot. For example, they can determine that a wider camera lens not only shows Michael Phelps, but the other swimmers as well, and the producer is more likely to linger over the wide-field shot because he knows that it still shows the leader (Phelps) in gloriously sufficient detail.

*This is akin to the astrophotographer knowing that he or she is imaging at the limit of their camera's ability from a resolution standpoint and has decided to trade off some detail for a wider field of view.*
