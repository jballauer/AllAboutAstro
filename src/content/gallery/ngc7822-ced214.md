<h2>About this Object</h2>
<p>You have to love the power of CCD cameras!</p>
<p>This photograph shows two distinctly different nebulae, NGC 7822 on the bottom and Cederblad 214 on the top. But through the "magic" of CCD technology, amateur imagers can now go so "deep" that it becomes difficult to know where one nebula starts and the other one leaves off! And because of that, there is often some confusion when it comes to identifying these two targets.</p>
<p>This nebular region can be found right at the Cepheus/Cassiopeia border. The easiest way to locate it would be to extend a line up from the center star (gamma cass) in the "W" shape of Cassiopeia until you hit the Cepheus border.</p>
<p>Though these nebulae are, in no way, small, they are difficult targets to detect visually. Apertures around 20 inches and dark skies are required, though many viewers report that an h-beta filter can make these much easier targets with much smaller, rich-field instruments. Approach this field like you would NGC 1499, the California Nebula, and it's likely you'll have success.</p>
